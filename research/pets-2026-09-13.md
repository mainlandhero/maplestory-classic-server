# Pets: permanent, all in the shop, and a type-3 body - 2026-09-13

The owner: *"Current, Brown Puppy, Panda and Dino Boy all have 3 day duration. Please edit the WZ if
needed to change all of them to permanent duration. Also please add all of the other pets into
the Cash Shop too ... They should also be permanent duration. They should never need to be
revived."*

Tags: **[L]** read off the client's bytes, its listing or a WZ leaf; **[I]** inferred; **[R]**
the reference server.

## 1. Where the "3 days" comes from

Not the shop rows: `Etc/Commodity.img` rows 126..128 (SN 160000000..2: Brown Puppy, Panda, Dino
Boy) say `Period 0`. **[L]** It is the pet item's own data, `Item/Pet/<id>.img/info/life`, in
days **[L]**:

```text
5000000 Brown Kitty  7     5000004 Black Kitty  90    5000008 Panda      3
5000001 Brown Puppy  3     5000005 White Bunny  90    5000009 Dino Boy   3
5000002 Pink Bunny   7     5000006 Husky         7    5000010 Dino Girl 90
5000003 Mini Kargo  90     5000007 Black Pig    90
```

The modern client's pet images (sixty sampled) carry the same keys plus a handful of newer
ones, and exactly one of them is permanent - `5000060.img`: `life 0`, `permanent 1`. **[L]**
That is the shape every classic pet is patched to (`tools/backport_install.py` step 4c). That
the classic client reads `permanent` is **[I]**; `life 0` is what the permanent pet ships with.

## 2. The eight missing rows

`CashShopCategory.img` category 5 "Pets" lists by `scope 600`, and `tools/dump_commodity.py`'s
arithmetic (`category = SN / 10000000 - 10`, `sub = (SN / 100000) % 100`, scope = category*100 +
sub) puts SN `1600000NN` under category 6 / sub 0 - the three shipped pet rows are exactly there.
So the eight pets get rows `160000003..160000010`, cloned from row 126 without its HOT badge:
Count 1, Price 100, Period 0, Gender 2, OnSale 1.

## 3. The pet item body

Buying a pet was refused at `Session::on_cash_shop_buy`: *"this server cannot build a type-3
item body yet. Sending one as a bundle killed the client on 2026-08-26."* The decoder is
`FUN_140304550`, dispatched for item type 3 by `FUN_1403095e0`; `tools/reads.py 0x140304550 2`
lists, after the shared base (`FUN_1403035a0`: itemId, hasCashSN [+ serial], dateExpire, u32,
u8) **[L]**:

```text
raw  u8  u16  u8  raw  u16  u16  u32  u16  u8  u32  u16  u16  u32
```

The reference server's `PetItem.encode` writes, after its base: `name (13)`, `level`,
`tameness`, `repleteness`, `dateExpire (FILETIME, 0 = no date dead)`, `petAttribute`,
`petSkill`, `remainLife`, `attribute`, `activeState`, `petHue`, `giantRate`, `short 0`,
`int 0` **[R]** - the same fourteen widths in the same order. `net::bag::pet_item_with_cash_sn`
builds that: the item's name, level 1, closeness 0, fullness 100, **dateDead = the
never-expires sentinel** (the "never revived" half), the rest zero. 67 bytes without a serial,
75 with. `item_blob` uses it for every `500xxxx` item, so the locker record, the locker-to-bag
move and the field-entry restore all carry it, and the three refusals are gone.

## 4. Not built, and not claimed

* **Summoning.** A pet that follows the character needs the pet pool's enter/leave/move
  packets (`0x...`, not read) and the double-click on a pet item to be answered. Nothing here
  does that; the plan says so.
* The meanings of the fourteen fields are the reference's; only the widths and order are this
  client's. If the client dies at purchase or at the move, the body is the suspect, not the
  data patch.
